# Brief 1: offering a free plot to the waiting list

*Notes pasted in after a planning call. The product is Mossbank, the app that community gardens use to manage plots, members and shared tools. Mossbank, the gardens and the people are made up for this exercise.*

From Ines (product), after the call with three garden coordinators:

- When someone gives up a plot, the coordinator goes through the waiting list by hand: emails the next person, waits, chases, then tries the one after. Coordinators said it "takes forever". One said plots sit empty "for weeks" in spring. Nobody had a number.
- Idea: when a plot is marked free, Mossbank offers it to the next person on the waiting list by itself. They get some days to say yes. If they say no, or don't answer, the offer moves to the next person.
- How many days? Ines said 7. Tomasz (coordinator at Fernside) said 3 is plenty. Not settled.
- Tomasz also wants to be able to skip the queue by hand "for good reasons", for example a neighbour of the plot who has helped out for years. Ines thinks that defeats the point of a fair list. Not settled.
- Someone on the call said "about 40% of offers get ignored". No one knows where that number came from.
- Offers go by email. Text messages came up, but we don't hold phone numbers for most people on waiting lists.
- Wanted "before the spring season". No date was agreed.
- Priya (engineering) asked what happens to someone who ignores an offer. Do they keep their place, go to the back, or come off the list? No answer yet.
- Mentioned in passing: a public page showing how long each garden's waiting list is. Ines: "nice, but a different piece of work".
